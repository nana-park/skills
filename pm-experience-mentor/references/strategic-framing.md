# Strategic career framing

The goal is to make experienced-PM expertise legible. Preserve the underlying experience while actively reconstructing its meaning, emphasis, professional vocabulary, and presentation. Do not merely polish the user's first description or stop at fact checking.

## Start with the signal to demonstrate

Use the target JD, interview question, or user's desired competency as the organizing lens. If it is absent, suggest the strongest plausible lens from the material instead of blocking on a questionnaire. Translate activities into decisions, constraints, evaluation criteria, and product consequences; avoid senior-sounding vocabulary without concrete meaning.

- A → B: Replace an activity label with a more revealing professional description when the actual work supports it.
- D → C: Bring a supporting experience into the story to demonstrate the target competency. Establish its real relationship to the main experience.
- E → C: Change the lens or detail selection: the same work may show discovery, prioritization, technical judgment, operational readiness, or localization.
- G → C: Distinguish the original trigger from the product meaning. A management request, event deadline, or personal interest need not be the headline; foreground the problem addressed and the user's contribution.

## Propose, then investigate

Offer a concise interpretation early: “이건 단순 운영보다 품질 기준을 정립한 경험으로 보여주는 게 더 강해요.” Explain why and ask one discriminating question. A conditional proposal is useful even before all details are known. Avoid a long list of questions or caveats that prevents the user from seeing the opportunity.

When materially different angles exist, offer two strong candidates and recommend one for the target role. Example: a launch task could be framed around validation design or cross-functional delivery; choose based on what the user actually decided and what the target audience values.

## Reconstruct the story, not the history

Combine related activities into an overarching problem → diagnosis → alternatives → decision → validation story when the relationship is supportable. Split a large project into competency-specific stories when useful. Reorder the presentation and promote a previously secondary detail to the headline. Preserve actual chronology, ownership, populations, and causal relationships; do not imply unrelated projects were one initiative.

The original motivation is not immutable wording. Prefer “상위 요청으로 시작한 과제를 사용자 문제 해결 관점에서 구체화했다” or “이 경험은 제품 관점에서 C를 검증한 사례로 설명할 수 있다” when appropriate. Only say “C를 목표로 설계했다” if that was actually a goal. If the user confirms that the new wording accurately captures their previously unarticulated intent, use it naturally without hedging every sentence.

Distinguish three working layers:

1. Established actions, decisions, constraints, and outcomes.
2. Defensible interpretation and professional reframing.
3. Proposed claims needing confirmation or additional work.

Layers 1 and 2 can support a strong final narrative. Layer 3 stays conditional until resolved. User approval of wording alone does not establish a new metric, experiment, authority, or result. When a preferred framing overstates the experience, provide the strongest supportable alternative, not just a refusal.

## Make sophistication concrete

Show the decision criteria, rejected alternatives, dependencies, and trade-offs that the work actually involved. Do not rename a demonstration as a controlled experiment or describe a PoC as production growth merely to sound senior. Clarify the actual validation design first and select the precise professional term.

An everyday description such as “문의들을 모아 개발팀에 전달했다” may become a problem-classification or prioritization story if the user identified recurring causes or set criteria. If they only relayed requests, explore adjacent work that supplies those judgments; do not quietly add them.

## Review the proposed framing

For a consequential rewrite, show the original, the recommended angle or sentence, the competency it demonstrates, and the one missing detail that matters. Confirm that the user could explain the underlying reasoning in an interview. Avoid treating speculative counterfactual alternatives as options actually considered at the time.

Keep the original available for comparison, but do not constrain the revised narrative to its wording, section order, initial goals, or initial classification.
