# PM Story Rubric

Use this reference for interim diagnosis and final synthesis. It guides judgment; it is not a mandatory form.

## Strong PM signals

- One concrete central problem anchors the experience; causes and solution-stage obstacles have clear relationships to it.
- An unfamiliar listener can follow the user difficulty, diagnosis, action choice, and result without filling in missing causal steps.

- A meaningful user or business problem was identified from credible evidence.
- The user's own judgment affected prioritization, scope, metric choice, solution choice, or iteration.
- Alternatives and trade-offs are specific rather than reconstructed after the fact.
- Metrics have definitions, limitations, and a believable relationship to the goal.
- Collaboration explains how decisions moved, not merely which teams attended.
- The feedback loop connects observation, diagnosis, change, and verification.
- Results distinguish observed outcomes from inferred impact.

## Common weaknesses

- A feature list replaces the problem and decision story.
- “We” hides the user's role.
- A result is presented without baseline, definition, or attribution.
- A proxy metric is treated as direct proof.
- The proposed solution appears without alternatives or selection criteria.
- Coordination is described as collaboration without tension, decision, or adaptation.
- Several distinct stories are forced into one narrative.

## Story classification

Treat classification as a choice of positioning, not a fixed property of the original draft. Explore alternative lenses and related supporting experiences before choosing the main signal for the target role.

Classify the experience by its strongest relevant signal. It may be:

- product discovery and diagnosis;
- prioritization and product judgment;
- experimentation and iteration;
- execution and operational readiness;
- cross-functional influence;
- platform or policy design;
- localization or market expansion;
- business development rather than a primary PM case.

Say when a story is useful but better placed as a secondary example. Do not downgrade it merely because it lacks a textbook structure; first investigate whether the relevant evidence exists.

## Synthesis shape

The original project's business trigger can be background while the user's product judgment becomes the headline. Distinguish that reframing from claiming a different historical goal.

Choose the structure that best exposes the actual judgment. A useful default is:

1. concise impact and context;
2. problem and discovery evidence;
3. goal and metric logic;
4. alternatives, judgment, and trade-offs;
5. execution and collaboration;
6. feedback loop and iteration;
7. result, limitation, and learning.

Include unresolved questions next to the affected claim. Do not hide them in a generic appendix.
