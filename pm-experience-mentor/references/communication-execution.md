# Communication and execution-plan evidence

Use this reference when communication, stakeholder management, delivery under constraints, or an execution plan may strengthen an experience.

## Find a communication incident, not a collaboration list

Do not summarize communication as “worked closely with marketing, design, engineering, and a vendor.” Find one incident in which different goals, constraints, information, or quality expectations threatened the outcome and the user changed what happened.

Start by asking the purpose of including the incident: which interview question or professional strength could it answer? Then recover the story one sentence at a time:

1. What was the concrete request or problem?
2. Which stakeholders saw it differently, and what constraint did each have?
3. What quality, schedule, resource, user outcome, or trust was at risk?
4. What did the user decide to preserve, trade off, scope out, or ask another team to provide?
5. What criterion triggered the decision or escalation?
6. What communication mechanism changed the work: a focused meeting, bundled feedback, shared artifact, review cadence, escalation path, or explicit ownership?
7. What changed, and what lesson or reusable principle is actually supported?

Ask only the next question needed to expose the missing link. Useful prompts include:

- “상대 팀이 정확히 요청한 건 뭐였고, 우리 팀에는 어떤 제약이 있었어요?”
- “그 요청을 그냥 거절하지 않고 받아들일 수 있다고 본 근거가 뭐였어요?”
- “어느 시점부터 일반적인 수정 요청이 아니라 에스컬레이션이 필요하다고 판단했어요?”
- “회의를 많이 한 게 아니라, 실제로 대응 속도나 품질을 바꾼 장치는 뭐였어요?”
- “본인이 지키려 한 것과 포기한 것을 하나씩 말하면 뭐예요?”
- “이 사건이 ‘개발자와 잘 소통한다’는 말보다 강한 이유는 뭐예요?”

## Extract the user's method

Once the incident is clear, identify the user's demonstrated playbook without inflating it into a universal principle. Examples of valid patterns include bundling review feedback to reduce rework, sharing policy and context while leaving design solution space open, escalating only after an observable quality threshold is repeatedly missed, or scoping a feature by both technical feasibility and the product's core user job.

Keep the concrete case attached to the principle. Prefer “세 차례의 서로 다른 채널 요청에도 목표 품질을 충족하지 못하자…” over “객관적인 기준으로 이해관계자를 관리했다.” Do not add a threshold, authority, result, or motive that was not established.

If the event also exposes a mistake, retain both lenses when useful: how the user recovered within the situation, and what should have been prevented earlier. Do not convert every failure into an unqualified strength.

## Compress communication for the upper layers

For Layer 4, preserve the stakeholder positions, sequence of attempts, exact decision or escalation criterion, artifacts, result, and limitation.

For Layers 1–3, use the smallest complete structure:

> incident and risk → the user's criterion or judgment → the decisive communication mechanism → result or lesson

Select the one incident that best proves the target competency. Do not give equal space to every stakeholder merely because each appeared in the project. If communication is secondary to the product decision, reduce it to the one or two facts that explain why execution succeeded.

## Turn an activity log into an execution plan

An execution plan is not every task in chronological order. Group work into stages with distinct purposes and decision gates. For each stage, recover:

- the stage objective;
- the main dependency or risk;
- the user's scope, priority, or resource decision;
- the validation method and participant;
- the signal that allowed the work to advance, change, or stop.

Use stages that fit the actual project. A case might move from product and policy design, to technical integration and external production, to internal quality validation, to a limited external release or customer-facing validation. Do not preserve internal acronyms merely because the user used them; name what the stage actually did and who participated.

Ask why a test existed, who the participants really were, and whether it was a usability test, QA, pilot, beta release, sales demonstration, or another form of validation. Correct the label when the activity's purpose and audience do not match it.

Keep one representative finding only when it proves the stage's value or the user's judgment. Omit low-value anecdotes from the upper layers even if they remain available in Layer 4. Separate verified outcomes from future business outcomes or plans.

## Final check

- Can an interviewer identify the conflict or execution risk without reading a stakeholder roster?
- Is the user's decision criterion visible, not only the communication channel?
- Did the mechanism plausibly change quality, speed, scope, or alignment?
- Is the execution flow grouped by purpose and validation gate rather than by every task?
- Are communication and execution concise enough that the central product problem and judgment still dominate the story?
