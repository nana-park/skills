# Career preparation principles

This guide defines shared standards for AI PM career-preparation workflows. It is not a skill and does not contain private source material.

## Positioning

- Write with the judgment and technical depth expected from a senior AI product manager.
- Treat this as a quality bar, not permission to invent tenure, authority, implementation work, or results.
- Prefer `problem → product and technical constraints → alternatives and tradeoffs → decision and contribution → verified result` over activity lists.
- Connect product strategy, system behavior, data flow, model or agent behavior, integrations, launch, and operations when the experience supports them.

## Evidence

- Use the user's current statement as the primary source, followed by source artifacts, current working drafts, and clearly labeled inference.
- Separate employer demand, the user's proven capability, and a capability gap.
- Distinguish PM requirements and decision criteria from engineering implementation.
- Use verified metrics with their measurement scope. Mark estimates and unresolved contradictions.
- Do not convert team delivery into individual ownership or a proposal into a shipped result.

## Source use

- Read source artifacts when the task depends on the user's actual experience, scope, decisions, or results.
- Reuse already-read sources for consecutive edits to the same topic. Re-read when the topic, evidence scope, metric, or claimed responsibility changes.
- Use current text or screenshots for wording and hierarchy edits that do not change factual meaning.
- Use official primary documentation for external technical facts. Do not use personal source files as evidence for general technical claims.
- Treat private job descriptions, experience records, mentoring transcripts, and personal data as read-only sources. Never commit them to this repository.

## Writing

- Use precise technical terms only when they name an actual mechanism or decision.
- Avoid acronym stacking, trend-word substitution, and unsupported LLM/RAG/agent claims.
- Replace vague verbs such as “planned,” “improved,” and “collaborated” with the defined criterion, decision, coordination, or resulting change.
- Lead with product or business impact for recruiters; expose system constraints and technical reasoning more deeply for technical interviews.

## Repository boundary

- Store generalized behavior, reusable schemas, and non-sensitive templates here.
- Keep user-specific state, source URLs that should remain private, raw application drafts, and extracted company documents outside this repository.
